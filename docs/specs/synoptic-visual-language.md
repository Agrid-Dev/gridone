# Synoptic visual language and symbol kit

- **Status**: Draft — revised for AGR-1391 (first review of the Okko plates, 2026-09-23): decisions 5, 6, 8, 11, 14, 15, 16 and 18 rewritten, 19 to 22 added. Revised again the same day for the ISO 16484 review: decisions 12, 13, 16 and 21 rewritten, 23 and 24 added, the *Standard references* section added.
- **Milestone**: M1: Use cases, data and specs (Synoptique project)
- **Issues**: AGR-1156 (this spec), AGR-1157 (vendor the kit, consumes the tokens), AGR-1159 (draw the symbols, consumes the sheets), AGR-1158 (projection, owns `project()`)
- **Sheets**: [`synoptic/kit/isometric.svg`](synoptic/kit/isometric.svg), [`synoptic/kit/flat.svg`](synoptic/kit/flat.svg), [`synoptic/kit/density-collector-8.svg`](synoptic/kit/density-collector-8.svg), [`synoptic/kit/plate.svg`](synoptic/kit/plate.svg), produced by [`synoptic/kit/generate.py`](synoptic/kit/generate.py) from the token values in `index.css`, which also refuses a palette whose colours sit too close. They follow `prefers-color-scheme`, so open them in a browser rather than an image viewer.
- **Tokens**: `apps/ui/src/index.css` (`--synoptic-*`, `--fluid-*`) registered in `apps/ui/tailwind.config.js`.
- **Out of scope**: the document format (`synoptic-document.md`), the projection function and depth ordering (AGR-1158), the editor (AGR-1165). The issue predates the format and the registry; where they disagree, the backend wins and the sheets follow it.

The document format says what a plate *is*; this spec says how it *looks*. Everything the format leaves to the renderer (colours, strokes, fonts, chips, arrows, what stale and faulty look like) is decided here, once, so the ECS view and the CTA view read as one product.

## Decisions

1. **Tokens, not hex.** The gallery kit's `COLORS` map (`src/lib/theme.tsx` in `agrid-scada-components`) is hardcoded hex on a dark ground with metal gradients. Every colour here is a CSS variable in `index.css` with a light and a `.dark` value, next to `--status-*` and `--hvac-*`, so a plate themes like the rest of the app. The kit never reads a literal.
2. **Fluids are keyed on the document's `Fluid` vocabulary**, one token per member of `synoptics.models.Fluid`, eleven in v1. The POC's three-colour grammar (primary red, ECS amber, bouclage blue) is kept for primary and DHW and extended to the rest. Fluids sit in a 45 to 55 % saturation band: they only need to be told apart from each other, the chip and the legend carry the identity, and it leaves saturation above 70 % to `--status-*`, so a fault outline is never the colour of the pipe running through it.
3. **A return is its supply shifted 18 points of lightness**, down in light, up in dark, hue and saturation unchanged. Measured rather than eyeballed: desaturating a return collapses the hue differences between returns (five returns became one brown band), so lightness alone carries the pairing. The generator enforces a CIE76 floor of 25 between every fluid and `--status-error` / `--status-ok`, 20 against `--status-warning` (a warning is an outline and a badge on a machine, never a run, and its `!` says it besides its colour; heating supply reaches 23.6 in the light theme), and 18 across the fluid set, in both themes; a palette edit that breaks it fails generation. Condenser water is violet rather than green for that reason: green is `--status-ok`. Bouclage is a light amber rather than the POC's blue: blue is reserved for cold water, and chilled loops take cyan.
4. **Fault and stale reuse existing tokens.** No `--synoptic-fault` or `--synoptic-stale` exists; see *Borrowed* under Tokens.
5. **Surface tokens and no literal**: the sheet keeps `--synoptic-plate` (the ground), `--synoptic-grid` (footprints, editor grid), `--synoptic-body`, `--synoptic-body-x`, `--synoptic-body-y` (the extruded glyph's faces, still used by a type the kit gives no volume) and `--synoptic-stroke` (glyph outline). The illustrated kit of the isometric view adds `--synoptic-edge` (the 1 px edge of every volume), three metal tones its gradients run through (`--synoptic-metal-hi`, `--synoptic-metal`, `--synoptic-metal-lo`), `--synoptic-brass-hi` / `-lo` (valves and the mixing valve), `--synoptic-vessel-hi` / `-lo` (the red expansion vessel), `--synoptic-dark` (motors, actuators, end caps), `--synoptic-slab-hi` / `-lo` / `-edge` (the slab under a group) and `--synoptic-reading` (a live value). Chips use `--card` / `--border`, text uses `--foreground` / `--muted-foreground`. Every token has a light and a `.dark` value, so a theme changes the tokens and every face, tube and slab follows. Chosen by review of the four Okko plates against direction 4 of the design canvas (AGR-1391).
6. **Gradients belong to the isometric kit, and nowhere else.** The sheet keeps its three tiers of line: silhouette 2 px in `--synoptic-stroke`; internal detail 1.25 px (fan, mesh, diaphragm) in `--muted-foreground`; operating information (chips, panels, labels) on `--card` with `--foreground` text. The isometric view draws each type as a volume shaded the way a brochure draws a plant room (`symbols/kit.tsx`): boxes with a lit top and a darker `+y` face, cylinders with a highlight a third of the way across, brass valves, a red vessel, dark motors, shadows on the floor. The gradients are declared once per plate (`symbols/defs.tsx`, `KitDefs`) with every stop a token from Decision 5, so "tokens, not hex" still holds. The first review (AGR-1391) called the extruded glyphs "sketchy" and asked for kit A of the canvas: this is that kit.
7. **Fixed footprints from the registry.** A symbol is drawn to the `Footprint` its type declares in `synoptics.symbols.registry.build_default_registry` and nothing else; ports sit on the face the registry says they leave through. The sheets draw the footprint under each symbol and mark each port with its name so a drawing that drifts from the registry is visible.
8. **Equipment outweighs pipework, and a pipe is a pipe.** A plate is read machine first, circuit second. A run is 6 px of fluid colour on a 10 px `--synoptic-plate` casing, so a run crossing another at a higher `z` reads as in front and a machine standing on the run breaks it; bends are elbows (4 px), not curves. The volumes outweigh the runs by mass, not by line weight. A run is static: no moving dash, whatever its `flow` reads. Animation is a state signal an operator must never depend on (it is off under `prefers-reduced-motion` and absent from a screenshot), and the first review found a partly animated circuit more confusing than none; the run state is on the machine (Decision 18). Circuit animation, if it comes, is a later decision.
9. **One set of flow chevrons per pipe, at the `to` end**, 9 × 9 px in the fluid colour, outlined in the plate colour so the head stands off the run. The format says `to` is where the arrow goes (Pipes, `synoptic-document.md`); a tee draws a 5 px disc of the trunk's colour at the branch point and no junction symbol.
10. **A value is a chip, never bare text.** 20 px tall, 4 px radius, `--card` on `--border`, value in 12 px semibold with tabular numerals, unit in 11 px muted after it. The label (`TT-05`) is 11 px uppercase muted above the chip. This is the shipped `ValueChip` of `glyphs.tsx` with a squarer corner and a label, so the AHU synoptic adopts it without redrawing.
11. **A tag hangs off its point on the run, wherever the search puts it.** A 2.5 px disc in the fluid colour marks the cell on the run; a 1 px muted leader joins the chip's nearest edge to it. The chip and its caption travel as one box, placed by the same search as a readout (Decision 14): 26 px off the point on the preferred side (above, or below when a body stands on the cells the chip would rise over), the other side next, then the diagonals and the sides, each ring 12 px further out, the first box clear of everything wins. The caption sits above the chip when the text is above the run and below it when it hangs under. A tag whose value is a literal of the document (`non mesurée`) is a note: muted italic text with no frame, so twenty of them no longer drown the four live values of a plate.
12. **Stale is a dashed chip in muted text, and it never blinks.** The chip border goes to `--muted-foreground`, dashed 3/2, the value and unit go muted; the label does not change. In a panel row the value goes muted and gains a 2.5 px muted disc before it. Nothing turns orange and nothing moves: stale is "this number is old", not a warning, and motion on a plate is reserved to an alarm nobody has acknowledged yet (a later decision, once alarms exist). Every reading carries the time the device last reported it: the chip's and the row's native tooltip says the caption, the value, that time and how long ago (`usePlateVocabulary.readingTitle`), and the popover writes it beside each point.
13. **Fault is the fault's colour on the symbol, by severity, not on the value.** `Device.is_faulty` belongs to the symbol's `device_id` (Decision 7 of the format), and the worst severity among its active faults picks the colour, after IEC 60073's meanings: `--status-error` (red) for an `alert`, `--status-warning` (amber) for a `warning`, `--muted-foreground` for `info`, which is for information and must not outshout the alert beside it (the faults table greys `info` for the same reason; blue is never used for a state, IEC 60073 reads it as "action required"). A faulty device whose severity is unknown reads as an alert. The symbol's silhouette is outlined in that colour at 2.5 px with a 7 px badge at the top-right of the footprint; its panel takes a 1.5 px border, the LED and the `DÉFAUT` value take the colour; a tag whose device is faulty takes it on the chip's border. On the machine (Decision 18) an alert paints the active part red, a warning amber, and an info fault leaves it to its run state: the badge says it. One module owns the four class tables (`fault.ts`); nothing else on the plate names a fault colour. Values are never recoloured on their own: a number is not a fault.
14. **Readouts are placed by one search, and never on a run.** The equipment panel is 144 px wide: title left, LED right, a rule, then one 18 px row per bound slot with the slot label muted left and the value right, `ÉTAT` and `DÉFAUT` first; a symbol with a single bound reading has a chip under its label instead. Placement is a ranked spiral search (`placement.ts`, `findSpot`): the spot hanging off the label first (a panel above it, a chip under it); else the rings around the body and its name, above, beside, below, then the corners, each ring 12 px further out, a step down the preference order costing 8 px, so text stays close and on the side it prefers when both are had. Obstacles are every body, bar, name, free label, tag and readout already placed, and every run as the *line* it draws (a box around a diagonal run is mostly empty plate, and forbade half the plate). The search walks up to 16 rings before it falls back on the label spot; on the four Okko plates no readout lands on a run. A displaced readout is joined to its symbol by a 1 px leader ending on the drawn body. Symbol names go through the same search before any reading (Decision 19). The device drawer beside the plate is gone: a click on a device symbol opens a popover anchored on the symbol, with the symbol's own points and a way to write the writable ones (Decision 21).
15. **Minimum text pitch is two cells, as a lint.** At one-cell pitch a chip is about 60 px wide and the projected step between neighbouring departures is 24 px, so tags on the same side cannot both sit next to their run. The editor (AGR-1165) may warn about it; the renderer no longer depends on it: the search of Decision 14 moves the second chip out, with a leader, rather than overlapping. A plate written through the API may break the pitch and still be valid.
16. **One plan glyph per type on the sheet, drawn after ISO 14617; one volume per type in the isometric view.** The flat sheet draws the plan glyph on its footprint, and the glyph is the graphical symbol of ISO 14617 (or its ISO 10628-2 example) the trade reads on a schéma de principe: the valve 2101 and the three-way valve 2103 with the motor actuator C0082 on a stem, the pump 2301 with its inscribed triangle, the check valve X8077, the vessel with dished ends 2062 for a tank, the plate exchanger 2516, the compressor 2302 inside a heat pump's frame, and so on: the *Standard references* table below lists every type with its registration number and what was adapted, and each glyph declares its reference in code. The isometric view no longer projects and extrudes that glyph: it draws the illustrated volume of `symbols/kit.tsx` for the type (a drum for a tank, a cabinet with a fan for a heat pump, a volute and a motor for a pump, a brass body and a handwheel for a valve, a standing sign for a link). The plan silhouette a run stops at and a fault outline wraps is the volume's (`SymbolDrawing.iso.outline`), so a run meets the drum of a tank rather than its footprint. Identity is carried by the volume in one view and the glyph in the other; a type the kit gives no volume still extrudes its glyph.
17. **Typography is the app's.** Figtree, with `tnum` on every value. Title 18 px semibold, caption 11 px semibold uppercase tracked, note 11 px regular, all mapping one-to-one to `Label.role`. Symbol labels are 11 px semibold, tag and slot labels 11 px semibold uppercase tracked. Nothing on the plate is smaller than 11 px: operating information is the tier an operator reads first, so it never drops below the caption size.
18. **Run state is on the machine.** In the isometric view the machine itself shows it: the heat pump's fan is green and turns while it runs (`--status-ok`, off under `prefers-reduced-motion`), grey when stopped, red when the device is faulty; a pump's motor carries a 4.5 px dot in the same colours, dashed and hollow while nothing is known; an isolation valve's handwheel is solid when closed, hollow when open, dashed and muted while silent, so a silent valve never reads as open; the loop heater's element glows in the heating colour while it heats. The label carries no LED there. On the sheet the LED after the label stays: `--status-ok` when the value is an on-state, `--muted-foreground` when off, `--status-error` when the device is faulty, fault first, and the isolation valve shows its state on the bowtie. The panel LED follows the same rule in both views. Nothing else moves: flow animation is gone (Decision 8), so a stopped machine reads as stopped in a screenshot.

19. **Names are placed, not pinned.** A symbol's name takes the kit's spot (above the volume; beside the tip of a link's arrow, where the plate's edge has room; on the face on the sheet) when that spot is clear of every run, every other body and every name placed before it; else the first clear spot around its body by the search of Decision 14, above first, with a 1 px leader back to the body once it stands off it. A collector's name lies along its bar, turned to the bar's axis, where nothing else is drawn. Names are placed before any reading, so a chip never covers one. The first review's "text crosses lines" was these names.
20. **Slabs under each group.** The document knows no zone, so the renderer reads the groups off the drawing: machines whose footprints are closer than three cells share a slab (`slabs.ts`), which reaches 0.7 of a cell past them and paints under everything, with a 6 px lip for thickness. Bars and off-page connectors are not equipment and stand on no slab. On the reference plate this yields the production, storage and distribution islands of direction 4 without a single change to the format.
21. **The page around the plate.** The plate is a card with a toolbar (a Plan / Isometric toggle that changes the view and never the stored document; zoom out, the zoom in percent of the fitted plate, zoom in, fit, full screen), a navigation panel that lists the named symbols with their run state and fault and locates one on the plate (search, hover rings the symbol and the reverse, selection centres the plate on it at 200 % and opens its points when it is a device), and a legend in the footer that explains everything the plate draws, each item drawn by the component that draws it on the plate so the two cannot drift: the fluids the plate carries, a reading in each of its four states (live, stale, silent, and an authored fact of the document), a fault at each severity, the sheet's run-state LED and the state nobody knows, a link to a missing view; and, on a second row, the key: the plan glyph and the name of every symbol type the plate draws, so the isometric kit reads to someone of the trade through the same key. Slot captions and type names are the page's words (`slots.*`, `types.*` in the synoptics catalogue: `ÉTAT`, `DÉFAUT`, `pompe à chaleur`), handed to the renderer as a vocabulary, never translated inside it. The wheel zooms the plate with no modifier; the page scrolls from outside it. A click on a device symbol opens a popover on the symbol: its name and a link to the device, the symbol's bound points with their live readings, and an inline editor for the points the device lets one write, through the same preflight and consent as the device page. The card index of plates is gone: `/synoptics` opens a plate (the pinned one, else the last seen, else the first stored) and the title's switcher reaches the others; an overview may return with the dashboard integration.
22. **A cell is 48 × 24 px and text never shrinks with it.** The isometric cell went from 80 × 40 to 48 × 24 px (flat: 48 to 40) so a bay fits a 1440 × 900 laptop at a text size an operator reads (Decision 17 keeps the 11 px floor; live values are 12 px in `--synoptic-reading`). Production chaud and froid are laid out too wide for any pitch to fit them legibly at once: the toolbar and the panel of Decision 21 are the answer, not a smaller font. Re-laying those plates is the authors' call.
23. **The renderer knows no locale.** Everything it writes that a page may translate (slot captions, the tooltip of a reading) arrives as a `PlateVocabulary`; without one it writes the registry's own names, humanised. The page builds its vocabulary from the synoptics catalogue (`usePlateVocabulary`); a spec holds the catalogue to the registry, so a type or a slot never shows as a key.
24. **Point presentation is value, unit, time and quality, together.** A reading is never a bare number: its chip or row carries the unit, its state (Decision 12) and, in the tooltip and the popover, the time the device last reported it; the device's fault, by severity, is on the symbol (Decision 13). This is what ISO 16484-3 calls point information presentation, as far as the plate shows it; the quality the server judges (a failed read, an expired value) will replace the client's age threshold once the API carries it.

## Tokens

Values live in `apps/ui/src/index.css` only, light and `.dark`, as `H S% L%` triplets like the rest of the file; a class is `text-fluid-dhw`, `stroke-synoptic-stroke`, a fill is `hsl(var(--fluid-dhw))`.

| Token | Used for |
|---|---|
| `--synoptic-plate` | plate ground, pipe casing, flow dash |
| `--synoptic-grid` | footprints, editor grid |
| `--synoptic-body` | top face of a body, duct fill |
| `--synoptic-body-x` / `--synoptic-body-y` | the two visible side faces of a body |
| `--synoptic-stroke` | symbol outline, glyph lines, port marks |
| `--fluid-<member>` | one per `Fluid` member, `_` written `-`: `dhw_loop` is `--fluid-dhw-loop` |
| `--synoptic-edge` | the 1 px edge of every volume of the isometric kit |
| `--synoptic-metal-hi` / `--synoptic-metal` / `--synoptic-metal-lo` | the stops of the kit's box, cylinder and tube gradients |
| `--synoptic-brass-hi` / `--synoptic-brass-lo` | valve bodies and the mixing valve |
| `--synoptic-vessel-hi` / `--synoptic-vessel-lo` | the expansion vessel |
| `--synoptic-dark` | motors, actuators, tube end caps, the meter's window |
| `--synoptic-slab-hi` / `--synoptic-slab-lo` / `--synoptic-slab-edge` | the slab under a group and its lip |
| `--synoptic-reading` | a live value, in a chip, a panel row, the popover and the legend |

`--fluid-cold-water` is not `--water`: same hue, but in the fluid band. `--water` means "liquid present at a probe" and keeps its own saturation.

Borrowed, not redefined: `--card` / `--border` (chips, panels), `--foreground` / `--muted-foreground` (text, leaders, stale, notes, info faults), `--status-error` (alert), `--status-warning` (warning), `--status-ok` (LEDs, the running fan and motor dot), `--fluid-heating-supply` (the loop heater's element while it heats), `--hvac-fan` (the distribution views' spinning fan).

On the TypeScript side (AGR-1157) the fluid map is a `Record<Fluid, string>` of literal classes next to `semanticColors.ts`, so Tailwind keeps them and no second naming appears.

## Geometry conventions the sheets draw to

Stated so the sheets can be checked against the registry.

- Isometric: 2:1 dimetric. `x` → right-and-down `(24, 12)` px, `y` → left-and-down `(−24, 12)` px, `z` → up `24` px per cell. A cell is a 48 × 24 px diamond (Decision 22). Text laid along an axis turns by `atan(1/2)`, 26.57°.
- Flat: one cell = 40 px, `x` screen-right, `y` screen-down.
- The pipe axis runs through cell centres at `z = 0.4`, so a run passes mid-height of a one-cell symbol and a port is the centre of the footprint face.
- Ports are marked on the sheets as a 3 px `--card` disc on the face, named as the registry names them, on a leader that clears the silhouette.

## Standard references

The plan glyphs of the sheet are drawn after the graphical symbols of ISO 14617 and their ISO 10628-2
examples, by registration number, which is what a plant schematic that follows ISO 16484-3 (which
references ISO 10628 for its schematics) is expected to use. The isometric kit A is illustrative by
choice (Decision 6); the legend's symbol key pairs each type's plan glyph with its name so a plate reads
to someone of the trade in either view. Each glyph declares its `standard.ref` in `symbols/drawings.tsx`;
`drawings.spec.ts` checks every declared reference appears in this table, and this table only says what
the code draws.

| Type | Reference | Drawn as, and what was adapted |
|---|---|---|
| `heat_pump` | ISO 14617 2302 | The unit's frame with the compressor of its refrigeration circuit inside (EN 1861 draws a packaged unit this way); ISO 14617 has no heat-pump symbol. |
| `tank` | ISO 14617 2062 | The vessel with dished ends, standing in its 1 × 2 footprint. |
| `mixing_valve` | ISO 14617 2103 | The three-way valve with the electric actuator (C0082, the `M` in a circle) on a stem, on the side opposite its third port. |
| `pump` | ISO 14617 2301 | The liquid pump: a circle with the triangle inscribed, apex on the circle downstream. |
| `pump_double` | ISO 14617 2301 | Twice, across the run. |
| `valve_isolation` | ISO 14617 2101 | The valve; solid when closed and hollow when open is a supervision convention, not the standard's. |
| `valve_control` | ISO 14617 2101 | The valve with the electric actuator (C0082). Its slot is a position, so it never fills. |
| `valve_check` | ISO 10628-2 X8077 | The check valve: the valve with a disc on its upstream apex. |
| `plate_exchanger` | ISO 14617 2516 | The plate heat exchanger: a frame crossed by both diagonals. |
| `air_separator` | ISO 14617 2039 | The vent to atmosphere on a separator's drum, where the air leaves; the trade's dégazeur. |
| `dirt_separator` | ISO 10628-2 X8031 | The gravity separator's settling cone under a separator's drum; the trade's pot à boue. |
| `expansion_vessel` | ISO 14617 2062 | A capsule with the membrane line, as the trade draws a vase d'expansion; adapted. |
| `energy_meter` | ISO 14617-6 instrument | The measuring instrument's circle on the run; the `kWh` mark names the register. |
| `loop_heater` | ISO 14617 2514 | The heat exchanger with coil-shaped tubes, electric as ISO 10628-2 X8132: a housing with the element's zigzag. |
| `link` | ISO 10628-1 off-page connector | The pentagon of an off-page reference. |
| `collector` | — | A header bar sized per instance; no standard symbol, none needed: it is pipework. |

Colours of state follow IEC 60073 as far as the plate tells states: `--status-error` (red) for an alert,
`--status-warning` (amber) for a warning, the muted tone for a fault that is only information, `--status-ok`
(green) for a machine that runs, the muted tone for one that is stopped; blue is never used for a state,
since IEC 60073 reads it as "action required" (Decision 13).

## Plate reference

[`plate.svg`](synoptic/kit/plate.svg) draws the ECS Est plate (`synoptic/ecs-est.json`) whole in the kit, with the app content area (1184 × 836 px: a 1440 × 900 laptop at 100 % less the 256 px sidebar and 64 px top bar) marked on it, with its names, values, states, panels, a faulty machine and the two readings no device exposes. It is the reference for Decisions 10 to 18 on a real plate, and what AGR-1159 draws against. The whole plate is 2210 × 1170 px, so the first real view pans or scales; that is AGR-1164's call, not this sheet's. The Ouest plate (`synoptic/ecs-ouest.json`) is the same template with a third tank column, and the hot production (`synoptic/production-chaud.json`, 3760 × 1761 px) is the first plate off it; neither gets a sheet of its own, the renderer's spec draws every committed plate.

The generator measures the faces the way it measures the fluids: at least 4 points of lightness between the plate and the top face and between adjacent faces, and at least 25 CIE76 between `--muted-foreground` and any face, in both themes; a token edit that breaks it fails generation.

## Distribution views

The shipped AHU and extractor glyphs (`apps/ui/src/components/synoptic/glyphs.tsx`) are kept as drawings and restyled: `--synoptic-plate` / `--synoptic-stroke` for glyphs and `--synoptic-body` for the duct instead of `fill-background` / `stroke-border`, the coil takes the fluid colour of what it carries (`--fluid-heating-supply`, `--fluid-chilled-supply`) instead of a caller-supplied class, the spinning fan keeps `--hvac-fan`, `ValueChip` and `MeasureTag` (label over value, leader line) become the chip and the tag. The pump synoptic shares the file and follows. The flat sheet shows the duct with damper, filter, fans, coils, chevrons and chips.

## Placement, in one place

Every piece of text a plate places (names, tags, readouts) goes through `placement.ts`: obstacles are boxes for bodies, bars, names, free labels and placed text, and segments (the drawn line, casing included) for runs. `findSpot` ranks the candidates of every ring by distance plus a side penalty and returns the first clear one. The renderer's specs probe the four committed plates for the invariants: no readout on a run, no name on a run, every displaced text joined to its owner by a leader, no two frames overlapping. When a plate breaks one, the plate is dense beyond what the search can serve and the author moves things; the renderer never hides a reading.

## Open points

- The fluid floor of 18 is what six hues reach in the dark theme; if a twelfth fluid comes, the check says whether the band has room.
- `energy_meter` is placed inline five times on the hot-production plate, which confirms it; `pump_double` is still placed by no plate, since each twin's heads are two devices on the instance and a symbol carries one `device_id`.
- Whether 11 px holds on a wall screen at distance is the first thing to revisit after the first real view; full screen (Decision 21) is the interim answer.
- Circuit animation (the whole active circuit moving, not the run of one machine) is deferred: the first review asked for it and then judged it too ambitious for v1.
- Production chaud and froid remain wider than a page at any legible pitch (Decision 22); re-laying them in the editor is open with their authors.
- A slab is inferred from proximity (Decision 20); if two groups are authored closer than three cells they share one. An authored zone in the format would settle it.
- The kit sheets under `synoptic/kit/` still draw the extruded glyphs at the old 80 × 40 pitch, and `generate.py` keeps its own Python copies of the plan glyphs, which now differ from the ISO glyphs of `drawings.tsx`; regenerating the sheets from the TypeScript glyphs (an export script) and at the 48 × 24 cell is open. The renderer's specs draw every committed plate, which is what checks the kit today.
- `--status-warning` (amber) is drawn on plates next to `--fluid-heating-supply` (orange): the generator now checks the pair at a floor of 20 (measured 23.6 in the light theme, above 25 in the dark one); whether the heating hue should move to reach 25 is open.
- The registration numbers of the *Standard references* table were read off the public ISO 10628-2 symbol sheet; the office copy of ISO 14617 should confirm them before the table is quoted in a tender.
- `plate.svg` draws readouts for free-standing symbols only; the marked chip on the bouclage pump and the panel on the loop heater, both inline, are the renderer's (its spec draws them), not the sheet's.
